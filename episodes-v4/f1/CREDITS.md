## Artworks and photography

- **Aristotle with a Bust of Homer** — Rembrandt (Rembrandt van Rijn), 1653. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437394). Rembrandt’s 1653 posthumous artistic representation, not an ancient documentary portrait or proof of doctrine.
- **Astrolabe of ‘Umar ibn Yusuf ibn ‘Umar ibn ‘Ali ibn Rasul al-Muzaffari** — ‘Umar ibn Yusuf ibn ‘Umar ibn ‘Ali ibn Rasul al-Muzaffari, dated 690 AH/1291 CE. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/444408). Actual 1291 instrument. Illustration of prediction/measurement, not a primary manuscript source for scholastic claims.
- **Chisel** — Unidentified maker, ca. 1473–1458 BCE. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/548778). Illustrative actual Egyptian chisel. No claim that it made the pictured medieval fountain or belonged to that workshop.
- **Pocket set of drawing instruments** — Peter Dollond, ca. 1755–60. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/198857). Actual photographed eighteenth-century drawing instruments; not claimed to have belonged to any philosopher.
- **The Drawing Lesson** — Thomas Rowlandson, 1780–1827. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/364029). A real historical drawing used to illustrate learning; no homunculus or miniature theater within a mind.
- **Fountain** — French, 13th century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/471044). Same actual medieval fountain photographed in a cloister. No construction stages, flowing-water footage or documentary maker attribution invented.
- **The Gardener** — Georges Seurat, 1882–83. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437656). Actual Seurat painting of a gardener; illustration of cultivation, not a philosopher portrait or a literal causal proof of a metaphysical claim.
- **A Goldsmith in his Shop** — Petrus Christus, 1449. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/459052). Actual painted artisan, customers, scales and coins. Financial context illustration, not a documented bank building or portrait of a philosopher. No saint identity is asserted.
- **Study of a hand** — Auguste Rodin, last quarter 19th–early 20th century. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/191861). Actual sculptural study of a hand. Created material artifact; not anatomy, a mental representation or an image of God.
- **Lunar eclipse of 2025 September 7 (Montage s3)** — Alpsdake, 8 September 2025 JST. Own work, CC0 1.0. [Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lunar_eclipse_of_2025_September_7_(Montage_s3).jpg). Four actual 2025 exposure cells are shown via nondestructive HTML crops in time order; the separate 2018 reference cell is excluded. - **The Music Lesson** — John George Brown, 1870. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/10240). Actual painting of flute instruction. Used for hearing, teaching and human action; no identification of the depicted people as philosophers or attribution of philosophical evidence to the painting.
- **River with a Distant Tower** — Camille Corot, 1865. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/435983). Corot’s studio-composed landscape, used for riverbank context; not a geographically exact documentary image.
- **Provence or French Rose** — Henri Joseph Redouté (French, 1766–1853), 1817–1824. The Cleveland Museum of Art. CC0 1.0. [Source](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1943.139). Canonical rose motif. Full branch crops exclude the plate credits. Catalogue title and inscribed title retained separately; no new species attribution.
- **Roses and Lilies** — Henri Fantin-Latour, 1888. The Metropolitan Museum of Art. CC0 1.0 / Public Domain (The Met Open Access). [Source](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/437996). Use the pink roses in the lower portion; the tall white flowers in the upper portion are lilies.
- **Saint Thomas Aquinas** — Carlo Crivelli, 1476. The National Gallery, London. Public domain (PD-Art / PD-old-100). [Source](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Saint_Thomas_Aquinas_%28Crivelli%2C_15th-century%29.jpg). Crivelli’s 1476 posthumous artistic representation of Saint Thomas Aquinas, not a portrait from life. Gold-ground crops are editorial material surfaces, not pictures of God.

## V5 — cutouts, object layers and editorial animation

The original museum rasters listed above remain unchanged. Original vector
silhouettes and component masks were authored locally against those rasters for
this project: ceramic jar, astrolabe body/central plate, hand sculpture, chisel,
fountain basin/pedestal, drawing-instrument case and compass legs, and the horse
subject. Painted details (drawing hand, flute, balance, working forearm, quill,
boat, flower and book page) are separate editable masks of the actual pictured
objects. Irregular editorial masks integrate the remaining painted fields; no
rectangular backing cards, generated images or invented historical footage are
used. Mask files and their hashes are recorded in
`composition/assets/art-v4/v5-cutouts-provenance.json`.

The canonical rose uses the exact original Cleveland Museum of Art 1943.139
raster, **Provence or French Rose**, with the plate inscription retained in the
source ledger. [Museum object and CC0 source](https://www.clevelandart.org/art/1943.139).
The shared master silhouette and six-part partition were prepared in the S01
production: stable base, main bloom, two buds and two compound leaves. All masks
share the full original 4331 × 5861 image plane. Their union reconstructs the
master silhouette without a duplicate whole plant underneath. Calyx/stalk pivots
remain at their actual source locations. Small finite articulations are editorial
movements of a historical botanical plate, not generated biological motion or a
growth time-lapse. The rose stays physically fixed in scenes about abstraction,
consideration and dependence; the explanatory attention or relation moves.
The local partition and pivots are in `composition/assets/art-v4/rose-parts-v5.json`.

The lunar transformation uses only Alpsdake's explicitly CC0 2025 exposures,
cells **02 → 07 → 10 → 20** from the verified montage. The 2018 reference image
is excluded. [Author's source and CC0 dedication](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Lunar_eclipse_of_2025_September_7_(Montage_s3).jpg).
The four real photographs share one fixed circular body, with curved editorial
reveal fronts. They are not rotated. This is an explanatory transformation of
actual still exposures, not a claim of continuous recorded eclipse footage or a
physical measurement between the exposures. The rejected NASA/Bill Ingalls
candidate remains absent from the active production.

Original local ornamental line work, small chapter-opening flourishes and
source-registered explanatory paths were authored for this project. They are not
replicas of historical source pages. The fountain's finite blue water-use trace
is an explanatory diagram of purpose; the museum photograph itself shows no
flowing water. The historic chisel is a tool illustration, not the identified
tool that made this fountain. The pharmacy jar's contents and efficacy are not
claimed. Painted people are not identified as additional historical philosophers.

V5 preserves all existing scripts, phonetic text, voice recordings, ASR anchors,
chapter/beat durations, fonts and audio mix. No new TTS, paid media generation,
render, screenshot or credit inside the video was added. The four additional
source review rounds and their limits are recorded element by element in
`research/polish-four-rounds-v5.json`; native technical checks are separate from
perceptual review of the live composition.
